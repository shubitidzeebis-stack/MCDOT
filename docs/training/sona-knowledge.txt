WHO QUALIFIES
- The company must be an LLC or corporation. An authority registered to a sole proprietor under an SSN cannot transfer, so that is not a fit.
- With Amazon Relay: an active Amazon Relay contract linked to the company, MC authority in good standing, safety rating Satisfactory or Unrated. Amazon DSP is a different program and does not transfer.
- Without Amazon Relay: MC authority and insurance continuously active for at least 180 days, insurance in force now, MC authority active and in good standing, Satisfactory or Unrated safety rating, clean violation history, no revoked authority.
- A lapsed or reinstated authority restarts the 180-day clock. The clock runs from the reinstatement date.
- Loans, liens and factoring do not automatically disqualify a company but must be disclosed; payoff is handled at closing. Financed trucks titled to the company can be a problem and need review.
- All owners must be available to sign.

WHAT DETERMINES VALUE
Age of the authority, safety record and out-of-service rates, insurance history, whether Amazon Relay is attached and its score, violation history, whether the company email and FMCSA phone number transfer, and the overall condition of the record. Trucks, trailers and customer relationships do not move the price.

FAQ
Can I sell just my MC or DOT number? No. FMCSA does not allow a bare MC or DOT number to be sold. The company that holds it is sold, and the authority goes with the company.

What transfers? The LLC or corporation with its MC and DOT records, the company email and phone if included, the company bank account, state and federal filings, and the Amazon Relay contract if there is one. Trucks and trailers only if separately agreed.

Why are the email, phone and bank account important? Amazon Relay and the broker verification systems tie them to the company record; changing them can suspend the account. The company email is required. The phone number can be excluded and the price adjusts slightly.

How does the process work? The seller provides an MC or DOT number. Veritor reviews the FMCSA record and checks fit. If the company qualifies, Lukas provides a written offer. Once the price is agreed in writing, both sides sign the bill of sale and exchange IDs. The acquiring carrier sends half the payment before the seller sends anything. The seller then sends the company documents. The second half is paid, so the seller is paid in full. Only then does the seller hand over logins. The acquiring carrier updates the state registration and takes over the bank account, and the seller is removed from it completely. Exact steps and written terms are explained by the team.

How long does it take? Typically 3 to 5 business days after an offer is accepted when everything is in order. If Amazon Relay onboarding, a bank account change, or a lender payoff is involved, one to two weeks is realistic.

Do I pay anything? No. The valuation is free and nothing is deducted from the price the seller accepts.

Is this a scam? Owners get many unsolicited calls, so caution is reasonable. With Veritor the seller has a written offer before any commitment, is paid in full before handing over any login, exchanges IDs both ways, can have their own attorney review the paperwork, and can meet the acquiring carrier in person if preferred.

My authority is inactive; can I add insurance and sell? Reactivating restarts the 180-day clock, so it will not be sellable right away unless it has an active Amazon Relay contract. The team can give the date it becomes eligible.

What about taxes? That is a question for the seller's CPA. Veritor does not give tax, legal or insurance advice.
