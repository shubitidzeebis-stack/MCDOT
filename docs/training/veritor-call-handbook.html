Module 01

What we are, and how to say it

Veritor Group helps owners of small US trucking companies sell their company. Not the MC number, the company: the LLC that the MC, DOT, email, phone, bank account and Amazon Relay contract all hang off. A separate buyer pays for the company. The seller keeps 100% of the number they accept. The buyer pays Veritor on the back end.

That last part is where nearly every call last month went off-script. Sellers ask "so you're a broker?" and the honest-sounding answer that came out was "technically we're an intermediary, we pair buyers with sellers, they pay us a commission." It was said in at least fifteen calls. It is exactly what the house rules forbid, because the words broker, middleman, agent, intermediary and "we buy" all create legal exposure the company is trying to avoid, and they make sellers suspicious of a spread being taken off their price.

The language rule

Describe Veritor only through what we do: we value the company, verify the record, prepare the paperwork, coordinate the buyer and the bank, and close. Never "we buy", "we purchase", "we're the buyer", "we sell it for you". Never broker, middleman, agent, agency or intermediary about Veritor, not even to deny them. If asked who is buying: "the buyer is a separate carrier; the structure gets walked through with Lukas before anything is signed."

Say this

"Fair question. We're not the buyer and nothing comes out of your number. We find the buyer, verify everything on both sides, prepare the bill of sale and walk both of you through the bank. You get the full price you agree to."

Not this

"Technically we're an intermediary. I've got a network of buyers and they give us a commission on the back end." (Aug 11)
"We buy logistic companies." (Aug 31)
"We pay maximum of $20,000 and we buy only the LLC." (Sep 9)

Who is who

  • Lukas (the site says "Luka S.") founded the company and is the only person who can make a binding offer. Sellers book valuation calls with him. On the phone line he is almost never mentioned, and when a seller asked, one agent described him as "in our marketing department". Do not do that. He is the founder and the decision-maker.
  • Donnie runs the calls this handbook is built from. Most of the good lines here are his. So are most of the mistakes, which is what happens when one person carries 150 calls a month with no script.
  • The front office drafts bills of sale, sends emails, runs the valuation pulls. Sellers experience their mistakes as yours: a wrong email typed in the front office nearly killed a $20,000 deal on Aug 26.

The one-line pitch

"You've got a company with an active MC and a clean record. There are carriers who will pay real money for that company because it saves them six months of waiting and a lot of paperwork. We find that carrier, check them, and run the handover so you're paid before you give anything up."

Module 02

Why anyone pays for this

You cannot sell an MC number. FMCSA says so, and Donnie's best line on the subject is the one to memorise: "per FMCSA regulations, it has to come with the business entity, whether it's the LLC or the corporation. You cannot just buy or sell a straight MC number." What buyers pay for is the matched identity: an LLC whose FMCSA record, email, phone, bank account and Amazon Relay contract all agree with each other and have history.

The Relay lock

Amazon Relay is the whole market right now. Buyers said no to every non-Relay company in September. Relay only lets a carrier onboard after 180 days of continuous active authority and insurance, and it checks that the bank account, email and phone on the application match the FMCSA record. If a new owner swaps in his own details, Amazon suspends the account. So a buyer cannot recreate what the seller has by starting fresh, and cannot take it over by force. He has to buy the company whole and change the record slowly, in the right order.

Aug 27 · outbound · Donnie
SellerI wonder why the new buyer just couldn't onboard with them if they had the current MC.
DonnieTo do that, it's quite a long process. If whoever onboards doesn't match what's on FMCSA, they'll just flat out suspend the account, because they don't know who this person is.
Why it works: one sentence explains the entire economics. The asset is the matched identity, not the number.

Who the buyers are

Fleet operators running fifty to a hundred trucks on Amazon, often out of Chicago. Amazon shuts a carrier account down after three or four violations, so they need a steady supply of clean, aged, Relay-attached companies to keep trucks rolling. A clean account that graduates to an A+ score unlocks Project Blocks rates. That is why an unused Relay account is worth less than a used clean one, and why a bad safety score can knock $4,000 off an accepted price overnight (it did, on Sep 9).

Say this to a seller as "carriers who need a Relay-approved company today rather than in six months". Do not say the buyer's city, fleet size, vacation plans or how many companies he has bought from us. All of that was said on recorded calls last month.

The same asset attracts fraud

Aged authorities are also what cargo-theft rings want: buy the company, leave the FMCSA record unchanged, book and steal ten loads under the old name, disappear. That is why $25,000 to $40,000 cash offers exist in the market and why sellers get 120 messages a day from overseas numbers. It is also why our pricing looks low next to the street number, and why the handover order in Module 06 matters more than the price.

Disqualify fast and with the reason. The best calls last month were ninety-second no's that left the seller grateful. The worst were seven-minute conversations with someone who could never sell.

In 148 calls, no one asked whether the entity was an LLC or a sole proprietorship, no one asked the safety rating, and no one asked who has authority to sign before quoting a price. The "not yet" seller

An authority granted in March is worth nothing in August and $13,000 to $15,000 in late September if the owner onboards Relay in the meantime. Give the date, give the number at the date, and give them the work to do. Donnie did this on Aug 24 and the seller volunteered to come back.

Module 04

The qualification call

Donnie runs the same script on nearly every call. It works because it is short, it is in a fixed order, and it ends by making the seller name their number first. This is the script with the three missing questions added.

  1. "You got a few minutes to answer some questions?" Permission gate. Then "What's your MC so I can pull it up on FMCSA?" and read the record while they wait.
  2. "Is this an LLC or corporation, and are you the only member?" New. Sole prop ends the call. Partners mean you need everyone on the bill of sale.
  3. "Does it come with the LLC, the email on FMCSA, and the phone number on FMCSA?" Email is required. Phone is worth $1,000 to $3,000 either way.
  4. "Do you have Amazon Relay set up?" Then: "Do you know your score, like an A or a B?" and "Which bank is attached to Relay?" If yes, ask for screenshots of the home, safety and performance pages before the call ends.
  5. "Any debts, liens, or loss runs?" Follow up: is it on the LLC or on you personally, is it truck financing, how much is left.
  6. "Are you set up on Highway, RMIS and MyCarrierPackets?" If they've never heard of them, they probably aren't. Explain: "the bigger brokers use these to verify carriers".
  7. "Do you have factoring tied to the MC still?" Which company, are you in a contract, can you get a letter of release, is there an early-termination penalty.
  8. "Did you register on Motus yet?" Say the URL exactly: motus.dot.gov. It was mangled as "modus", "modis" and "notice.gov" on calls and sellers could not find it. Half of carriers do it in fifteen minutes, half take two to four weeks. The original owner must do it before anything on the record can change.
  9. "How much do you pay per month for insurance?" Logistics, not price. It tells you whether a lapse is coming.
  10. "Have you had any other offers?" New. Ask it here, not after ten minutes. One seller had a competing buyer's plane ticket booked before the qualification finished.
  11. "How much are you looking to get?" Always last. Let them anchor, then correct with a fact from the record (Module 05).

Read the record aloud

Sellers trust what you read off FMCSA more than what you say. "Twelve inspections, that's good. The out-of-service rate on the vehicle side is high, that'll affect it." "It says reinstated 12/29/2023, so it goes by the reinstatement, not the year you opened it." Read the date, the inspections, the OOS percentages and the insurance status every time.

Sep 8 · outbound · Donnie · asking for proof without offending
DonnieIf you could just send me the screenshots of the home and the safety and performance page. Not saying you're lying to me, but the buyers still want to see and confirm.
SellerNo, no. You always verify. Always.
DonnieWe've had problems in the past where people say they've got what they don't, and then we go through all the hoops and it doesn't turn out that way.
Why it works: pre-empts offence, attributes the ask to the buyer, and the seller ends up endorsing the policy.
Before you hang up

Tell them exactly what happens next and when you will call. "I'm going to take this to the buyer side today. You'll hear from me by tomorrow afternoon either way." Five sellers last month called back angry because "nobody called me". A promised email that never arrived cost us a $20,000 deal.

How to anchor

Ask their number last, then correct it with a fact they didn't know about their own record. Never argue about value.

DonnieAmazon A-plus doesn't even go for $50,000.

Why it works: he doesn't argue, he corrects a fact. The seller can't dispute his own FMCSA record.

Solve the objection instead of discounting for it

The best negotiating moves last month were not price moves. A buyer wanted $23,000 off because the email didn't convey; the seller offered to create a new email and hand it over and kept the price. An owner couldn't afford the $1,100 premium due before close; Donnie got the buyer to cover it as a clause and the deal closed at $14,000 the next day. An owner didn't want to lose personal mail; the answer was a forwarding clause plus $2,000 for the cleanup time.

Who says numbers

You may quote the website range and explain the levers. You may say "with Relay this usually lands around $14,000 to $16,000". You may relay a buyer's number word for word with the reason attached. You may not invent a number, and you may not bid against yourself. On Aug 24 one call went "would $15,000 work?", "would $20,000 work?", "I can try for $26,000" in four minutes without a buyer ever being consulted, and the seller walked anyway. Only Lukas makes an offer, and the site promises it in writing.

Do not say

"That's just a standard valuation" about the website number. It undercuts our own product. Say what drives it instead.
"I'll see if I can get rid of it to somebody." It is a man's company.
A number above the engine's ceiling. $26,000 and $28,000 were entertained on Aug 24 with no rating verified.

Module 06

The transfer, step by step

This is the sequence as practised on every closing last month. Learn the "they give a little, you give a little" explanation word for word: it is the single best asset in 148 calls, and it is the honest answer to "is this a scam".

Aug 19 · outbound · Donnie · the ladder
DonnieWe structured it so they give a little, you give a little, they give a little, you give a little. The general email logins, the FMCSA login, and the Amazon login are what's really important for you. The documents are nice to have, but without those three things the buyer doesn't have anything. So the seller is paid in full before they give those up. It's a protection thing.
Why it works: it explains the sequence by explaining the seller's leverage, so the structure sells itself.
  1. Price agreed. Relayed from the buyer with the reason attached. Lukas confirms it in writing.
  2. Prep work, if needed. Motus registration by the original owner. Factoring release letter. Loss runs. FMCSA email or address corrected. Relay onboarding (one to two weeks). If the Relay bank is not Chase, Bank of America or Wells Fargo: the seller opens a Chase business account in the LLC's name, gets the bank confirmation letter "with the stamp in the middle", attaches it to Relay, waits one or two days for Amazon to accept.
  3. Bill of sale (Module 07). Drafted by the front office from the seller's ID, sent to the company email, reviewed, e-signed by both parties. IDs exchanged through us. Clauses go in here: forwarding personal mail, buyer covers a premium, seller stays available for Motus, buyer's timeline for the ownership change, no new debts after the sale date.
  4. Bank details. Seller gives account name, bank, main-branch address, routing and account number. Not by text to a personal phone. See Module 12.
  5. First half of the money. Wire or Zelle. Zelle may come in blocks over two days for a first-time payee.
  6. Documents. Articles of organization, letter of authority, EIN letter, COI. Only after the first half lands. "Hold off on the documents until you get the first payment."
  7. Second half. Seller is paid in full.
  8. Credentials. General email login, FMCSA login with password and PIN, Amazon Relay login. Verification codes get relayed live during the handover.
  9. Bank handover. One of four ways: seller adds the buyer as an authorized signer and the buyer removes the seller (fastest, Chase only); both make simultaneous bank appointments and the banks talk; buyer flies the seller to his bank and covers the trip; buyer or his rep flies to the seller. Bank of America only works in person.
  10. Phone port, if included. "Call your carrier, give them your account number, say you need a PIN to port the number. Text me the account number and PIN, I give it to the buyer, he calls, done."
  11. Buyer's post-close work. Own insurance on within two to three days (seller's falls off). Secretary of State updated to remove the seller. EIN handled. Relay ownership change over two to four months. Do not close the old bank account until told: Amazon may see it and suspend.
Aug 31 · outbound · Donnie · the "no attachment" close
DonnieYou'll have the signed bill of sale. You'll have the bank transferred out of your name 100% into the buyer's name, and Chase will confirm this. Third, Secretary of State gets updated with his information. Fourth, EIN gets updated. After that you have absolutely no attachment to the company.
Why it works: four countable, verifiable facts. Sellers fear residual attachment more than they fear the price.

The FMCSA record question

Buyers are asking to leave the FMCSA ownership record in the seller's name for two to four months, because changing it suspends Relay. Sellers spot the risk instantly ("if there's a fatality claim, it goes under my liability"). The website says records are updated after closing. The legal file marks this as attorney question number one and unresolved. Until Lukas settles it: explain the buyer's request honestly, say it is written into the bill of sale with a no-use clause and a deadline, and do not tell a seller "it's not legally possible for anything to come back on you". That is a legal opinion, and it was given three times last month.

Website versus phone

The site promises a purchase agreement before sensitive documents, funds through a closing attorney's escrow released on signature, no credentials before closing, and a 3–5 business day close. On the phone the practice is a bill of sale, buyer-to-seller payment in two halves, credentials after full payment, and one to two weeks when Relay or a bank change is involved. Both are defensible on their own terms. Do not promise the website version on a call you cannot deliver, and do not tell a seller escrow exists when it does not. Module 16 lists this for Lukas.

Module 07

The Bill of Sale

The bill of sale is the only document in the deal. It is what the seller signs, what the buyer's money is conditioned on, and what a seller reads line by line at the kitchen table before signing. On Aug 19 one did exactly that, for eleven minutes, and every question she asked is answered below. It is generated in our admin by the front office from the lead card; you feed it the terms, read it before it goes out, and explain every section on the phone.

What it is

A one-document Membership Interest Purchase Agreement. The seller conveys 100% of the membership interest in the LLC to the buyer. That one line is why FMCSA allows the sale at all: the company changes hands and the authority goes with it. It is not an asset sale and it is not an MC transfer, and you should say so if a seller calls it either. The generator draws it in three designs (Masthead, Instrument, Term Sheet) with identical wording; pick Instrument for a cautious buyer who is about to wire money.

Section by section, in the order it prints

  1. Sale of Membership Interest. "Seller hereby sells, transfers, assigns and conveys to Buyer 100% Membership Interest in the Company, representing all outstanding membership interest." If there is a partner, the front office adds a second seller and the document grows a third signature column. Every member signs or the sale is not complete.
  2. Purchase Price. One number. The generator prints a flagged PENDING chip when it is missing, precisely so nobody can miss it. A document went out on Aug 18 with the price and the payment terms both pending; the seller noticed before we did.
  3. Payment Terms. Three options. The default reads "the full purchase price shall be paid by Buyer to Seller after all Company documents have been delivered and all Company access has been transferred to and confirmed received by Buyer". The second is paid in full at an in-person meeting. The third is Staged: an initial payment, then the remaining balance "upon delivery of all Company documents and transfer of all Company access". If you promised the ladder on the phone, the document must say Staged with the two amounts filled in. See the gap below.
  4. Insurance (optional). Five arrangements the front office can pick: none; a deposit for insurance deducted from the price; a down payment refunded in full if Amazon Relay is denied; the buyer obtains their own policy and adds their own bank before transfer; the buyer pays the upfront premium by a stated date, failing which the seller can treat the document as void. Match the phone to the paper: the $1,100 premium a seller could not afford in August is what the deposit and deadline options are for, and "Relay pending" deals get the refundable one.
  5. Company Information. Legal name, DBA, address, phone, email, MC number, USDOT number, interest transferred. The email printed here is the mailbox that changes hands. Read it aloud to the seller before they sign.
  6. Seller Information and Buyer Information. Legal names and addresses, taken from the IDs exchanged through us. The buyer directory behind the generator stores a name and an address only; no government-ID data is kept, so do not paste ID numbers into the notes.
  7. Seller's Deliverables. Nine standard lines: MC certificate; FMCSA portal login and DOT PIN; insurance certificate and COI; company primary email access; company primary phone number; Amazon Relay access; loss runs; driver files and ELD records; other documents the buyer reasonably requests. The email and phone lines print the actual address and number, for example "Company primary phone number ((352) 232-3706) - transferred to Buyer". That rule exists because a personal number once went onto a bill of sale as the company phone, and the Florida deal in Module 08 shows exactly how it happens: FMCSA had the company line, Relay had the owner's personal cell, and "company primary phone number" nearly meant her cell. If the phone is excluded, the line comes off and Excluded Assets goes on.
  8. Financial Responsibility Before Closing. Two paragraphs. The seller warrants the company is free and clear of all debts, taxes, tolls, tickets, penalties, violations, loans and insurance balances at closing; and everything incurred before the closing date is the seller's, the buyer assumes none of it. This is the section sellers circle. The honest explanation: "anything from before the sale date is yours, anything after is his. That's why we ask about factoring and liens at the start, so nothing surprises either side at this line."
  9. Additional clauses. The clause library holds six: Cooperation Period (30 days of reasonable cooperation, no new liabilities either way); Post-Sale Obligations (seller completes the Secretary of State transfer, EIN and insurance/BOC-3 cooperation, buyer files everything after); Buyer Travel and Insurance Costs; Excluded Assets (trucks, trailers, company bank accounts and balances, personal property); Indemnification and Dispute Resolution (binding arbitration in the company's state, prevailing party recovers fees); Proof of Transfer (written proof the company is out of the seller's name within seven days, with the updated FMCSA record). Anything else is typed in as a custom section. Each one becomes its own numbered section before Governing Law.
  10. Governing Law. The laws of the state in which the company operates.
  11. Signatures. Seller, buyer, and a co-seller column when there is one. E-signature from a phone is fine. When a buyer wants a notary, the August deals used an online notary service: the whole file is uploaded, one signer per session, and each party does their own.
  12. Wire transfer instructions. The seller's bank name, account name, routing and account number print on the document itself. That is where bank details belong: collected by the front office for the paper, not texted to an agent's personal phone. If the buyer pays by Zelle, the name on the receiving account must match the seller's name on the document.
Paper versus phone

On the phone the ladder is "you're paid in full before the logins move". On paper, both the default term and the Staged term say the money, or the balance, is paid after documents and access are delivered. A seller who reads carefully will see the two disagree, and the Florida seller did: "shouldn't there be a blurb saying first half up front, second half after documents and logins?" Until the library has a payment term that matches the ladder, ask the front office for Staged terms with both amounts and a custom section that spells out the order: first payment, then documents, then balance, then credentials. Module 16 lists this for Lukas.

Clauses real deals needed that are not in the library

  • Forwarding personal mail. The buyer forwards anything personal that lands in the transferred mailbox. Asked for on Aug 18 and again later in the month; type it in.
  • No use of the seller's private Amazon account. When Relay is paired to the owner's shopping account, the buyer agrees not to purchase on it. Asked for on Aug 19; type it in.
  • No-use period while the record stays in the seller's name. The buyer keeps the FMCSA ownership record unchanged for the Relay ownership change, does not operate under the authority meanwhile, and completes the change by a date. This is the FMCSA record question in Module 06 and attorney item one in Module 16. Write the buyer's request down honestly, put the date in, and let Lukas review the wording. Do not pair it with Proof of Transfer, which promises the opposite within seven days.
  • Availability window. Cooperation Period says thirty days. Buyers asked for four months on the Florida deal. Put the number the buyer actually needs in the clause, and tell the seller the same number on the phone.
  • Buyer covers a cost. Insurance premium, travel, the seller's time to clean up an email. The insurance options cover most of it; travel is in the library; cleanup time is a custom line with a dollar figure.

Walking a seller through it

Read it in print order, one sentence per section, and answer every question with a fact about the document rather than reassurance. This is the Aug 19 walk-through, edited only for the banned words.

DonniePerfect. Then it's disclosed, and the buyer's already said it's not an issue.

Never

Never send a document with a PENDING chip on it. Never let the buyer draft or edit the bill of sale; document control is the seller protection we sell. Never put a personal phone number or a personal email on the deliverables without the seller reading the line first. Never take bank details by text; they go on the paper. Never promise a clause you have not confirmed with the front office is in the document.

The deal sheet

Agents cannot open the generator. What the front office needs from you, in one message, so the document is drafted right the first time: legal company name and DBA; MC and USDOT; the FMCSA email and phone, and whether each conveys; seller's legal name and address as on the ID, plus any partner; buyer's legal name and address; price; payment terms (staged amounts if the ladder was promised); insurance arrangement if any; deliverables to exclude; extra clauses in plain words with any dates and dollar figures; the seller's bank details for the wire panel; effective date; which design. Module 09

Objections

The answers below are the best real ones from last month, edited only to remove banned words and other sellers' details.

"Is this a scam? How do I know you're real?"

Answer with the sequence, not with reassurance. "You'll receive half the payment before you send anything. Then you're paid in full before you send the logins, which are the only thing the buyer can't get without you. IDs are exchanged both ways, and you can meet the buyer in person if you'd rather." One seller who had been scammed forty times accepted on that alone.

"You're a broker, right?"

Module 01. Do not accept the label and do not deny it with a synonym. "We're not the buyer and nothing comes out of your number. We find the buyer, verify both sides, prepare the paperwork and run the handover."

"My company is worth $150,000 / I've had offers of $35,000"

"Buyers don't price off revenue or customers. They price off what's on FMCSA: the age, the inspections, the out-of-service rate, and whether Relay is attached. An A-plus Amazon company doesn't go for $35,000." If they have a real offer above the market, tell them to take it. Do not be sarcastic about it.

"What about my trucks and my customers?"

"The buyers already have equipment rolling. What they want is the company and its record. Trucks and trailers would need a separate buyer, and customer relationships don't move the number." Say it in the first three minutes, not after ten.

"I'll just wait for it to age"

"At five years it might bump $3,000 to $5,000. The big money for aged MCs is ten, fifteen, twenty years. In the meantime you're paying insurance on it." Honest, numeric, and it closes the door without pressure.

"I'm not giving up my email / my phone number"

Phone: "That's fine, we'll exclude it. It's worth about $2,000 to the buyer, so the number comes down a little. Some buyers keep the number the same." Email: "The email has to come with it. If it changes on FMCSA, Amazon suspends and brokers freeze the account for months. Three options: set up forwarding, clean it up before close, or we write a clause that the buyer forwards anything personal. If cleanup takes your time, we can ask the buyer to make that up in the price."

"My name stays on FMCSA for months? That's risky."

"You're right to ask. The buyer keeps the record as is until Amazon's ownership change goes through, and the bill of sale carries a no-use clause and a deadline for that. I'll have Lukas walk you through exactly what's in writing." Then stop. Do not give a legal opinion.

"Why do you need my bank account?"

Name it before they raise it. "Amazon ties the payment account to the company. If the buyer swaps in his own, Amazon shuts it down. So the account transfers with the company, and Chase is the easiest bank for that. There are four ways to do it and you're removed from the account completely."

"Can we do it all at once? I'll notarize everything."

"The two-step protects both sides. You're paid in full before the logins move, and the buyer isn't wiring the whole amount before anything moves. If you'd rather do it in person at the bank, that's one of the options." Do not say "we've had sellers take the money and run".

"Why would anyone want Relay? It's the worst-paying freight."

Do not let it go with "yeah". "The buyers run big fleets on it, and a clean account that gets to A-plus can bid on Amazon's contract lanes. Amazon shuts accounts down over violations, so they need a supply of clean companies."

"Is my company listed for sale somewhere? Highway flagged me."

Module 11. This is an incident, not an objection.

"What about taxes?"

"That's a CPA question and I'm not going to guess at it. Generally an equity sale is treated differently from an asset sale, and your accountant will know which applies." Nothing more.

Module 11

When it goes wrong

"My company is listed for sale online and Highway flagged me"

Two separate sellers in two weeks. Highway saw the company circulating as a $18,000 listing, put the carrier under investigation, and the owner could not book a load for two weeks. Donnie's diagnosis on the call: the MC had been passed to a second intermediary who passed it on again. This is the highest-priority operational problem in the data and it cost sellers real revenue.

Until Lukas fixes the leak: never share an MC outside our verified buyer list, never confirm to a buyer that a company is "for sale" in writing, and treat confidentiality as asset protection, not a courtesy. If a seller reports a flag: stop, apologise for the situation without admitting a cause you do not know, take down the exact wording Highway used, escalate to Lukas the same hour, and give the seller a time by which they will hear back. Do not say "I won't move forward on this anymore" and hang up. Do not promise a written takedown confirmation you cannot send.

Scam panic mid-deal

A seller who was scammed earlier saw example Amazon screenshots and thought the buyer was already in her account. Answer the mechanism: "It's not possible, he doesn't have the password yet. Those screenshots are examples I send so you know what Amazon will ask for." Then "anytime you have a concern, call me and we'll sort it immediately". She was fine in four lines.

Phishing email "from DOT"

"That's not official. FMCSA mail comes from a dot.gov address. Save it, call FMCSA, they'll probably reset your password and issue a new PIN." Help even when the company is unsellable. That seller blessed him a hundredfold.

Post-payment holdout

One seller took full payment and then demanded more money before releasing the email. It happened because the half-and-half ladder was not used. The ladder is the fix. If it happens anyway, escalate to Lukas; do not phone third parties about the seller, and do not threaten court. A call last month did both, to a notary who turned out not to be the seller's lawyer.

Personal life fused to the business

Relay paired to a personal Amazon shopping account. Company email is also the family email. Company phone is the owner's cell. Google Workspace lapsed and the email is dead. Each of these appeared more than once. Ask about all four during qualification, offer the forwarding clause, and get Lukas involved before promising a fix.

Dropped calls and bad lines

The complete payment-and-credential sequence was delivered to one family in four broken pieces from a parking lot on Sep 8. Never explain closing mechanics on a bad line. Call back from the office and follow with the document-list text.

Wrong seller, wrong file

Another company's phone number was read aloud on a $20,000 close. An offer meant for someone else reached the wrong seller. Screenshots went to the wrong number for a whole call. Confirm the company name at the top of every call and re-read the thread before you send.

Module 12

Lines you never cross

Every item below happened on a recorded call or text last month. None of them was malicious. All of them create exposure for the company or the seller.

  1. Never coach a misrepresentation. A seller was told to declare a box truck he doesn't own on the Relay application and to swap a stranger's VIN onto his insurance policy so the COI would flow to Amazon. That is insurance fraud and Amazon fraud. If the equipment doesn't qualify, the company doesn't qualify.
  2. Never give legal, tax or financial advice. "It's not legally possible for anything to come back on you." "You have no case in court." "Move the $150,000 of debt off the LLC into your personal name." Each was said last month. Route to Lukas or tell them to ask their attorney or CPA.
  3. Never make an offer. "Would $12,000 work?" is an offer. Relay the buyer's number with its reason. Lukas confirms in writing.
  4. Never discuss other sellers or buyers. Not their names, cities, fleet sizes, deal history, or the one who "took the money and ran". Each of those was shared at least three times.
  5. Never comment on anyone's nationality or accent. "If they're calling with an Indian accent it's probably not a good sign" was said to a seller on a recorded line. It is discriminatory and it is a liability. Warn about unverified buyers by what they do (no ID, no paperwork, ask you to keep insurance in your name), not where they're from.
  6. Never take credentials early or by SMS. No logins, PINs, passwords or 2FA codes before full payment, and never by text. "I can't change an email address when I don't have anything signed" was a seller's line, and she was right.
  7. Never promise a date you can't control. "Wrapped up by Friday, money in your pocket." "Same day." "Ten to fifteen minutes." Every one of these slipped. The site's line is "typically three to five business days after an offer is accepted"; with Relay onboarding or a bank change, say one to two weeks.
  8. Never tell a seller to hide the sale from a lender or factoring company. "Don't tell them you're selling" was said twice. A UCC lien is a lien; the release letter is the path.
  9. Never blame sellers as a class. "The only problems I've ever had are with the sellers." Said to a seller.
  10. Never guess a fact. Motus URL, the IRS form number, whether an email change suspends Relay, whether you can only have one MC per state. Say "let me confirm and call you back", then do.
  11. Never let the buyer draft the bill of sale. Document control is the seller protection we sell.
  12. Never touch a seller's or a third party's account. Probing a seller's email against Amazon before any agreement, or resetting an ex-dispatcher's Relay login through the seller's inbox, were both considered last month.

Module 15

Self-check

Answer out loud before opening each one.

1. A seller says "so you're basically a broker between me and the buyer?"

Don't accept the label, don't deny it with "intermediary". "We're not the buyer and nothing comes out of your number. We find the buyer, verify both sides, prepare the paperwork and run the handover. You get the full price you agree to."

2. FMCSA shows "reinstated 12/29/2023". The seller says the company opened in 2021. What is the age for pricing?

About twenty months at the time of writing. The clock runs from reinstatement. Read the line aloud, explain it, and if they dispute it, offer to help them check with FMCSA rather than shrugging.

3. A seller wants $30,000 and has an A-plus Relay account, Chase bank, clean record, phone included.

That is the top of the market: $20,000 to $23,000 in practice, $25,000 engine ceiling with a Satisfactory rating verified. Say "an A-plus company with everything included is the best case, and those have been landing around $20,000 to $23,000. I'll take it to the buyer side and Lukas will put the number in writing." Do not say $30,000 is possible.

4. The seller's Relay bank is Navy Federal.

Not transferable. Seller opens a Chase business account in the LLC's name, gets the stamped confirmation letter, attaches it to Relay, waits one to two days for acceptance. Then the authorized-signer route works.

5. A hotshot operator's trailer doesn't qualify for Relay. A buyer suggests he apply as a box truck and VIN-swap the buyer's truck onto his policy.

No. That is a misrepresentation to Amazon and to the insurer. The company doesn't qualify for the Relay path as it stands. Price it as non-Relay or decline.

6. The seller texts "here's my password, code is 714".

Stop the channel. "Let's not do passwords over text. I'll call you and we'll do the handover live once the second payment has landed." Nothing credential-related moves before full payment.

7. "My name stays on FMCSA for three months? What if there's an accident?"

"You're right to ask. The buyer keeps the record as is until Amazon's ownership change completes, and the bill of sale has a no-use clause and a deadline for that. Lukas will walk you through exactly what's in writing." Do not say it's legally impossible for anything to come back on them.

8. A seller calls: Highway flagged his company as "for sale online" and he can't book loads.

Incident. Take the exact wording, don't guess the cause, don't deny in writing, escalate to Lukas the same hour, give the seller a time you'll call back, and call back at that time.

9. The seller declines $18,000 for a local buyer's $15,000 because "I don't want to wait six months".

Correct the misconception before accepting the loss. "There's no six-month wait on our side. With the bill of sale signed you're paid in halves over a couple of days; the months are the buyer's Amazon paperwork, not yours." That deal walked over a fact nobody corrected.

10. Five days after you quoted a number, the seller texts "nobody called me".

You broke the "before you hang up" rule. Call today, apologise once, give the real status, and give a date. "I'm just waiting for somebody that wants it" is not a status.

Module 16

Open items for Lukas

These are gaps between what the website promises, what the legal file says, and what happens on the phone. New hires cannot resolve them and should not paper over them. Each one showed up in the calls.

  1. Escrow and the purchase agreement. The site promises a closing attorney's escrow and a Membership Interest Purchase Agreement before sensitive documents. Every closing last month used a bill of sale and direct buyer-to-seller payment. Either the site changes or the process does.
  2. "No credentials before closing." Credentials move after full payment but before the Secretary of State, EIN and FMCSA updates, sometimes months before. Define what "closing" means in writing.
  3. The FMCSA record. Buyers leave the seller as owner of record for two to four months to protect Relay. The legal file calls this attorney question number one. Sellers are asking about liability on every call and getting improvised legal opinions.
  4. Timelines. "3–5 business days" on the site versus one to two weeks in practice, and "three to four months" for the ownership change. Sellers hear both and one of them sounds like a lie.
  5. Who makes offers. The site says written offer from Lukas. On calls, numbers are named by whoever is on the line, including "we pay maximum of $20,000" from an agent introducing himself as Luca. The house language rule is not reaching everyone who answers the phone.
  6. The buyer-network leak. Two sellers flagged by Highway in two weeks after their MC was passed on beyond our buyer list. This one is costing sellers money now.
  7. Sensitive data channel. Bank details, IDs, passwords and 2FA codes are moving by SMS to a personal phone. Designate a channel.
  8. Licensing. The legal file flags unresolved state business-broker licensing questions. The safe posture is introduce, do not negotiate price, do not custody funds. Calls currently negotiate price on every deal.
  9. Pricing outside the engine. $26,000 and $28,000 were discussed with no safety rating verified; a 50% driver OOS company was quoted $15,000 against an $8,000 floor. Decide whether the engine is the rule or a guide.
  10. Stale "400+ closings" claims in three blog posts. The verified figure is 10+ since June 2026.
  11. Payment terms on paper. The phone promises "paid in full before the logins move"; every payment term in the generator says the money or the balance is paid after documents and access are delivered. A seller read the difference aloud on Aug 19. The library needs a term that matches the ladder, or the ladder needs to match the paper.
  12. Blank documents. A bill of sale with the price and payment terms pending reached a seller on Aug 18. The generator flags pending fields; someone still has to look before it goes out. Decide who signs off on every document that leaves the front office.
