If your LLC doesn't yet hold an Amazon Relay contract, the qualification line is this: the
MC authority needs **at least 180 days of continuous history**, with continuous insurance
over that same window, before it's in range for a written offer.

This isn't arbitrary. It maps to how Amazon Relay onboarding works.

## The Amazon onboarding window

Amazon's logic for accepting a carrier into Relay weights several factors, but two of them
are decisive: the **age of the MC authority** and **continuous insurance coverage** over
that period. Amazon wants to see a seasoned carrier — enough operating history to show the
authority hasn't lapsed, hasn't gone dark, and hasn't accumulated the kind of violation
pattern that shows up early and gets worse.

Under about six months old, an MC hasn't had time to establish that track record. Amazon's
risk model doesn't have enough signal yet, so fresh authority sits outside the onboarding
window until it ages into it.

## What happens before day 180

A written offer isn't off the table, but it won't qualify for Relay onboarding yet.
Depending on your situation, there are two paths:

1. **Keep the authority active and insured until it crosses 180 days,** then submit. The
   most common path, and often the simplest.
2. **Get a written valuation now on other terms.** A fresh MC without Relay can still have
   value in the right circumstances — [requirements](/requirements) covers the full picture.

## What 180 days actually means

The count starts from the date the MC is granted active status by FMCSA — not the date you
applied. If you're not sure when that was, your FMCSA portal shows it. The insurance clock
has to run continuously alongside it; a lapse resets the streak.

## When it doesn't apply

If your LLC already has an active Amazon Relay contract, the 180-day rule doesn't apply at
all. The contract itself carries the qualification; the MC age isn't a factor.
