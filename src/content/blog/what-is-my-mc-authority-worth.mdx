The first question every seller asks is &ldquo;what&rsquo;s it
worth?&rdquo; The honest answer is &ldquo;it depends&rdquo; &mdash; not
because we&rsquo;re hedging, but because there are five or six real
variables that move the number meaningfully.

Here&rsquo;s the framework we actually use when pricing an LLC.

## The variables

In rough order of how much they swing pricing:

1. **Active Amazon Relay contract.** This is the biggest single
   factor. An LLC with an active Relay contract trades in a much
   higher band than a non-Relay LLC, because Relay onboarding is the
   bottleneck most acquirers are trying to skip.

2. **MC authority age.** For non-Relay LLCs, authority needs at
   least 180 days of continuous history to be in range for a written
   offer &mdash; Amazon&rsquo;s onboarding criteria wants to see that
   track record. Authority younger than that hasn&rsquo;t seasoned in
   yet, and the value follows accordingly.

3. **Insurance status.** Active in-force coverage is always
   preferred. For non-Relay LLCs it&rsquo;s usually required.

4. **Violation history.** Clean FMCSA records are worth a premium.
   Out-of-service orders, HOS pattern violations, or unsafe-driving
   thresholds reduce the price &mdash; sometimes substantially.

5. **State of registration.** Some states are friendlier than others
   for trucking operations. This is a smaller factor but it shows up
   on the margin.

6. **Active loans or factoring lines.** Doesn&rsquo;t change the
   underlying value of the LLC, but it changes the wire to you. The
   purchase price is the same; what you net is different.

## What we don&rsquo;t price for

A few things sellers sometimes expect to add value, but actually
don&rsquo;t much:

- **Branding and Google reviews.** The business is typically
  re-branded after closing. Your Google reviews are tied to your
  business name and aren&rsquo;t worth much in transfer.
- **Driver roster.** Drivers are at-will. They don&rsquo;t come with
  the LLC unless they choose to.
- **Office furniture, signage, marketing assets.** Out of scope.
- **Existing load-source relationships.** Helpful but not
  load-bearing. Most of these re-establish quickly under new
  ownership.

## What increases the number

- **Long, clean operating history with consistent revenue.** Even
  when an LLC isn&rsquo;t being valued primarily for cash flow, a
  track record of clean operation is worth a small premium.
- **Pre-existing equipment in good condition.** If trucks or trailers
  transfer with the LLC and they&rsquo;re in operating shape with
  clean titles, the price adjusts up.
- **Proactive disclosure.** Sellers who flag everything up front get
  a smoother, faster close. Honesty itself doesn&rsquo;t add a
  premium, but a faster close has its own value.

## Why we don&rsquo;t publish a price grid

We&rsquo;ve thought about putting numbers on a public page. The
reason we don&rsquo;t: any grid is a misleading simplification.
Two LLCs with the same MC age, same insurance, same state can have
twenty-percent different valuations because of one variable that
doesn&rsquo;t fit cleanly in a table (recent violation, equipment
condition, lender complications).

The right way to price an LLC is to look at the specifics, run the
analysis, and write a number. That&rsquo;s why our quotes come back
within hours instead of being published as a grid.

## How to get the highest number

Three things, in order of impact:

1. **Have an active Amazon Relay contract.** Biggest single lever.
2. **Keep insurance active and current.** Even if you&rsquo;re going
   to sell soon, don&rsquo;t let it lapse mid-process.
3. **Disclose proactively.** A clean diligence pass means the offer
   doesn&rsquo;t shrink later.
